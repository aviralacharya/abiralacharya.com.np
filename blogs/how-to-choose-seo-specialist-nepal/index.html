How to Choose an SEO Specialist in Nepal

A good hiring decision usually comes down to six areas: relevant experience, technical SEO capability, search-intent understanding, verifiable evidence, transparent deliverables, and business communication. You do not need an SEO professional who can recite hundreds of SEO terms. You need someone who can look at your website and connect technical decisions, content, search demand and conversions to the way your business actually makes money.

Start by asking whether the person has worked on websites similar to yours. A trekking company may need destination and itinerary content, commercial landing pages, internal linking and international SEO. A restaurant in Kathmandu may depend heavily on local search visibility, its Google Business Profile, reviews, location relevance and conversion paths. An ecommerce store has a completely different technical problem set involving categories, products, filters, duplicate URLs and indexation. Relevant experience does not mean the specialist must have worked in exactly your industry, but they should understand the search behaviour and technical constraints that matter to your business.

Then ask how the specialist proves their work. A statement such as "I increased traffic by 300%" tells you almost nothing without a baseline, timeframe, source and explanation of what was changed. A useful case study should tell you where the website started, what problems were found, what work was implemented, how long the process took and which metrics changed. Google Search Console is particularly useful because it provides search performance data such as clicks and impressions, while analytics can help connect organic traffic with behaviour and conversions.

Finally, examine the proposed working relationship. Who performs the implementation? Who writes content? Who approves changes? How often will you receive reports? Who owns Search Console and Analytics? What happens if the strategy needs to change? These questions may sound administrative, but they can determine whether your SEO investment remains useful after the contract ends.

First, Decide What You Actually Need From SEO

Before contacting an SEO expert in Nepal, write down the business problem you want SEO to solve. "I need more traffic" is a starting point, not a complete objective. A restaurant might actually need more calls and direction requests. An ecommerce store might need more non-branded product discovery and completed purchases. A trekking agency might need more enquiries for specific trekking packages from international markets. A local service company might need more qualified leads from Google Maps and organic search.

Different objectives lead to different strategies. If your website has serious indexing problems, publishing fifty new articles is unlikely to be the first priority. If your technical foundation is healthy but your commercial pages do not satisfy search intent, another technical audit may not solve the main problem. If you already rank for informational queries but receive few enquiries, the problem may be page targeting, commercial intent, conversion design or the mismatch between traffic and your actual customers.

A useful first brief can be surprisingly simple. Define your main services or products, your target locations, your most valuable customer actions, your important competitors and the pages you believe should generate business. Also identify whether your priority is local visibility, Nepal-wide visibility, international traffic, ecommerce sales, leads, bookings or recovery from a technical problem. An experienced SEO consultant should be able to turn this information into a prioritised roadmap rather than simply handing you a list of keywords.

This distinction matters because SEO is not one universal activity. Local SEO Nepal requires different work from international SEO. Ecommerce SEO requires different architecture decisions from a five-page professional services website. A hotel needs to think about destination intent, accommodation queries, local relevance and bookings, while a SaaS company may care about problem-based searches, product-led content and international competition. The first sign of a strong provider is often not the recommendation itself, but the quality of the questions they ask before making one.

What Does an SEO Specialist in Nepal Actually Do?

An SEO specialist in Nepal can work across several layers of a website, from technical infrastructure to content and conversion paths. The exact scope depends on the business, but a serious SEO engagement normally involves diagnosing how search engines discover, understand and evaluate a website and then improving the parts that matter most.

Technical SEO

Technical SEO is the foundation that allows search engines to crawl, interpret and index your website efficiently. An SEO professional may inspect crawling, indexation, canonical URLs, XML sitemaps, robots.txt, redirects, broken links, duplicate pages, JavaScript rendering, site architecture, mobile performance, Core Web Vitals and structured data. These are not technical exercises performed simply to make an audit look impressive. A canonical problem, for example, can affect which version of a URL Google considers the primary page, while poor architecture can make important pages unnecessarily difficult to discover.

A good technical SEO expert in Nepal should also explain priorities instead of dumping hundreds of issues into a spreadsheet. A missing meta description on a low-value page is not necessarily more important than an important service page accidentally carrying a noindex directive. Similarly, a website may have a few performance warnings that are worth improving without those warnings being the main reason its commercial pages are not visible. Ask the provider to distinguish critical problems, important improvements and optional recommendations.

Technical SEO also becomes more complicated when websites use frameworks such as Next.js, Laravel or heavily JavaScript-driven interfaces. The specialist needs to understand how the website actually works rather than applying a WordPress checklist to every project. Google provides documentation covering search appearance, structured data and other technical aspects of Search, so a provider should be comfortable explaining recommendations in terms of how search engines access and understand content.

Keyword Research and Search Intent

Keyword research is not simply finding phrases with high search volume. The more useful question is what does the person actually want when they search this phrase, and which page should satisfy that intent? Search intent can broadly be informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational or local. Someone searching "Everest Base Camp packing list" is looking for information, while "Everest Base Camp trek price" may be comparing commercial options. Someone searching for "trekking agency Kathmandu" has a much stronger local-commercial signal.

This is why a competent SEO professional should map keywords to pages instead of attempting to make one page rank for everything. A service page should usually serve service-related commercial intent, while supporting articles can answer specific informational questions and link naturally to the relevant commercial page. Creating a separate blog post for every variation of the same keyword can create unnecessary duplication rather than useful topical depth.

When interviewing a provider, ask them to take three or four keywords from your business and explain which URLs they would target. The answer is more revealing than a large keyword spreadsheet. Listen for reasoning around intent, competition, existing page authority, business value and the current condition of your website. On-Page SEO and Content Strategy

On-page SEO covers how individual pages communicate their purpose and usefulness. Titles, headings, content structure, internal links, images, URLs, contextual information, author information and conversion paths all contribute to the page experience. A good SEO professional should improve these elements without turning the page into a collection of repeated keywords. My guide to on-page SEO in 2026 covers the current checklist in detail.

Content strategy goes beyond publishing frequency. One useful approach is a hub-and-spoke structure, where a comprehensive primary topic is supported by focused pages covering closely related questions. For example, a travel website could have a hub called Complete Everest Base Camp Trek Guide, supported by pages about Everest Base Camp Trek Cost, Everest Base Camp Packing List, Best Time for Everest Base Camp Trek, Everest Base Camp Difficulty, and Everest Base Camp with Helicopter Return. The supporting pages can link contextually to the hub and, where appropriate, to each other.

This structure gives readers a logical path through a topic while helping search engines understand relationships between pages. It also creates opportunities to capture different stages of the search journey without forcing one page to answer every question. When evaluating a content strategist, ask not "How many blogs will you publish?" but "How will you decide which pages deserve to exist, what intent they serve and how they will connect?"

Local, E-commerce and International SEO

Local SEO matters when customers search for businesses, services or places within a geographic area. Google Business Profile is particularly important for eligible local businesses because profiles can appear in Google Search and Maps with information such as location, contact details, hours, photos and reviews. A local SEO professional may work on the profile, website location signals, service pages, reviews, local relevance and structured data, but should avoid creating dozens of near-identical city pages simply by replacing Kathmandu with Pokhara, Biratnagar or Bhadrapur. Google explicitly treats doorway-style pages targeting similar regions as a form of search spam. My step-by-step guide to optimising your Google Business Profile for local customers in Nepal shows what compliant local visibility work looks like.

Ecommerce SEO introduces another layer. Category pages, product pages, filters, faceted navigation, duplicate URLs, canonicalization, product information, internal linking and structured data all need to work together. A provider who has only worked on brochure-style websites may not automatically understand ecommerce indexation problems.

International SEO is different again. A Nepali company targeting Australia cannot simply translate or slightly modify a Nepal-focused keyword list and expect the same strategy to work. Search behaviour, terminology, competition, location relevance and commercial expectations can vary between markets. Ask whether the provider has experience researching the specific market you want to enter.

These labels describe working models more than universally fixed job definitions. An SEO freelancer may personally handle strategy and implementation. An SEO consultant may focus heavily on diagnosis, strategy and guidance while your internal team or developers perform implementation. An agency normally provides a broader team structure, potentially covering SEO, content, development, design or digital PR. The actual scope can vary considerably between providers using the same title.

How to Check an SEO Professional's Experience

Do not stop at a portfolio page. Ask the provider to explain what changed, why it changed, how it was measured and what part they personally contributed. A real SEO case study should have a starting condition. If the website had 1,000 organic clicks and later had 2,000, the useful information is not just "100% growth." You want the timeframe, data source, work performed and context surrounding the change.

A practical framework is:

RESULT = Metric + Baseline + Time Period + Source + Attribution

For example, "organic clicks increased from X to Y over X months according to Google Search Console after technical and content changes" is much more informative than "traffic doubled." Even then, attribution should be handled carefully. SEO rarely operates in a vacuum. Seasonality, brand campaigns, website redesigns, paid advertising, new products, public relations and market changes can all influence performance.

Ask whether the provider can walk through an actual URL. Which problem did they identify? What did they change? Was it technical, content-related, structural or off-page? How did they validate the change? What happened afterward? Someone who has actually implemented SEO should generally be able to discuss the reasoning behind their work without hiding behind vague terminology.

What a Real SEO Case Study Should Contain

A credible SEO case study should contain enough context for another person to understand what happened. Ideally, it identifies the type of business, the initial problem, the diagnosis, the strategy, the implementation, the timeframe, the measurement source, the outcome and the limitations. It does not need to expose confidential client information, but it should still contain meaningful evidence.

Be cautious with screenshots that show a dramatic upward graph without labels, dates or data sources. A screenshot can be genuine and still fail to demonstrate that the SEO provider caused the improvement. Ask whether the result came from organic search, whether branded and non-branded traffic were separated where relevant, and whether the provider can explain the work behind the graph.

The strongest case studies also discuss what did not work. SEO involves testing, prioritisation and changing assumptions when evidence says something different. A provider who can explain a delayed result, an unsuccessful content direction or a technical constraint may give you more useful information than someone whose portfolio contains only perfect upward graphs. Questions to Ask an SEO Specialist Before Hiring

Before signing a contract, ask questions that force the provider to explain their process rather than sell you an outcome.

  1. How will you audit my website?
    Look for a process covering technical SEO, content, search intent, competitors and business priorities.
  2. What will you prioritise first?
    A strong answer should explain impact, effort and business relevance rather than simply listing every issue.
  3. How do you conduct keyword research?
    Listen for search intent, competition, relevance, commercial value and page mapping.
  4. How do you map keywords to pages?
    The provider should be able to explain why a particular keyword belongs on a service page, category page or article.
  5. What technical SEO issues do you normally check?
    The answer should go beyond titles and meta descriptions and include crawling, indexing, canonicals, redirects and architecture.
  6. Who will implement the changes?
    Clarify whether the SEO provider, developer, content team or your staff will do the work.
  7. Who writes and reviews the content?
    Ask how subject-matter expertise and editorial quality will be maintained.
  8. How do you approach backlinks?
    Ask how relevance and quality are evaluated and whether paid or manipulative link schemes are used.
  9. How do you measure SEO success?
    Look for business outcomes alongside search metrics.
  10. What will the first 30 days look like?
    You should receive a realistic roadmap rather than a promise of immediate rankings.
  11. What should I expect by 90 days?
    Look for measurable work completed, technical improvements, content progress and learning, not guaranteed rankings.
  12. What happens if the strategy does not produce progress?
    A good answer should involve diagnosis and adjustment.
  13. Can I see an anonymised report?
    This helps you judge whether reporting is actually useful.
  14. Who owns Search Console and Analytics?
    Your business should retain control of its core accounts.
  15. What SEO practices will you refuse to use?
    The answer can reveal how seriously the provider treats search quality and long-term risk.

What Should an SEO Specialist Deliver in the First 90 Days?

The first 90 days should establish a foundation, implement priorities and create enough evidence to refine the strategy. It should not be presented as a universal ranking timetable because websites, markets and competition differ.

First 30 days may include a technical SEO audit, Search Console and analytics review, keyword research, keyword-to-page mapping, competitor analysis, critical technical fixes and a prioritised roadmap. The objective is to understand the website before scaling activity.

Days 31–60 may move into implementation: improving important service or category pages, fixing internal linking, addressing structured data, improving local SEO where relevant, publishing or revising priority content and resolving technical issues identified during the initial audit. If developers are involved, this is also where implementation dependencies become important.

Days 61–90 can focus on supporting content, topic clusters, additional internal links, technical validation, performance analysis and strategy refinement. Search Console data can help identify pages and queries gaining visibility as well as opportunities that need further work. The exact deliverables should be agreed in advance because a newly launched website, a mature ecommerce site and a local restaurant will not require identical 90-day plans.

How Much Does SEO Cost in Nepal?

There is no single meaningful "correct" SEO pricing in Nepal. The cost depends on website size, competition, industry, technical complexity, number of pages, target locations, content requirements, implementation responsibilities, link acquisition, reporting and the expertise involved.

SEO may be offered as a one-time audit, a project, a monthly engagement or a broader ongoing growth programme. Local SEO can have a different scope from ecommerce SEO. A technical migration project may require concentrated specialist work for a limited period, while a competitive international campaign may require continuous content, technical improvements and authority development.

When comparing proposals, do not compare only the monthly number. Compare:

PRICE → SCOPE → SENIOR TIME → DELIVERABLES → IMPLEMENTATION → REPORTING → BUSINESS VALUE

A cheaper proposal can contain fewer implementation hours, fewer pages, less strategic work or almost no content. A more expensive proposal may include substantial technical implementation and content production. The useful question is not "Which quote is cheapest?" but "What exactly am I receiving for this investment, and does it address my business problem?"

SEO Red Flags to Watch Before Hiring

One of the clearest red flags is a promise of a specific Google ranking. Search performance depends on many factors outside an SEO provider's direct control, so a responsible professional should explain strategy, measurement and uncertainty rather than sell certainty.

Other warning signs include guaranteed traffic, unrealistic timelines, secret methods, keyword stuffing, copied content, mass low-quality backlinks, automated link schemes, private blog networks, vague monthly reports and providers who refuse to explain what work is actually being performed. Google identifies practices such as keyword stuffing, manipulative link schemes and scaled content created primarily to manipulate rankings as spam-related practices.

⚠️

Watch out for scam pricing: A provider offering hundreds of articles or backlinks at an unusually low price is usually cutting corners on quality control. Google treats large quantities of unoriginal, scaled content — whether produced by generative AI or any other method — as scaled content abuse. More pages do not automatically mean more search visibility.

Another red flag is a provider who controls all your accounts. If you cannot access your Google Search Console, Analytics, Business Profile, domain, hosting or CMS independently, you have created unnecessary business risk. An SEO professional needs access to work; they do not need to become the permanent owner of your digital assets.

Who Should Own Your SEO Accounts and Assets?

Your business should retain ownership or administrative control of its important digital assets. That includes your domain, website hosting, CMS, Google Search Console, Google Analytics, Google Business Profile, content, business email and advertising accounts where applicable.

The SEO professional should receive the access level necessary to perform the work. For example, Google Business Profile supports separate owner and manager roles, allowing businesses to give other users access without handing over the entire account. This is a much healthier arrangement than giving an outside provider your primary password and hoping the relationship remains positive forever.

This matters particularly for local businesses. Your Business Profile can appear in Google Search and Maps and contains important business information, reviews and customer-facing details. Google also provides mechanisms for businesses to manage ownership and access. Your SEO provider should be a trusted operator within your account structure, not the gatekeeper of your business identity.

How SEO Results Should Actually Be Measured

Rankings are useful, but they are not the complete picture. A business should consider organic clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, non-branded visibility, landing-page performance and technical health alongside actual business outcomes such as qualified leads, calls, enquiries, bookings, purchases and revenue where reliable attribution is available.

For local businesses, Google Business Profile actions can also matter. Depending on the business, customers may interact with the profile through calls, directions, website visits or other available actions. Google describes Business Profiles as a way for eligible businesses to manage their presence across Search and Maps.

Traffic alone can also be misleading. Suppose organic sessions increase substantially but the additional visitors are searching for information unrelated to your products or services. The traffic graph looks positive while the business sees no meaningful improvement. This is why the SEO provider should connect search visibility to the pages and actions that matter commercially.

A good report should therefore answer three questions: What changed? Why did it change? What are we doing next? A spreadsheet containing rankings without interpretation is not the same thing as strategic reporting.

What SEO, AEO and Generative Search Mean for Businesses in Nepal

SEO focuses on improving a website's ability to appear and perform in organic search. That includes technical accessibility, useful content, search intent, site structure, internal linking and other signals that help search systems understand the website.

AEO, or answer-oriented optimization, focuses more explicitly on making useful information clear and easy for answer systems to interpret. This can involve concise answers, well-structured content, clearly defined entities, supporting context and strong relationships between related topics.

Generative search adds another layer because search experiences increasingly combine information from multiple sources. Google currently documents AI features as part of its Search appearance ecosystem, alongside traditional search features and structured data guidance.

That does not mean businesses should abandon conventional SEO and chase every new acronym. A strong foundation still matters: accurate business information, technically accessible pages, useful original content, clear entities, trustworthy information and a website that genuinely answers customer questions. No SEO provider should promise that a particular AEO or generative-search tactic will guarantee citations in an AI system.

For a Nepali business, this becomes particularly useful when building content around real expertise. A trekking company can demonstrate practical knowledge of routes, seasons and logistics. A hotel can provide useful information about its property and destination. A professional service business can explain real customer problems rather than publishing generic articles simply because a keyword tool suggested them.

Why First-Hand Experience Matters When Choosing an SEO Specialist

SEO is not just an exercise in changing title tags. Websites have real technical constraints, business priorities and people responsible for approving changes. A useful SEO professional should be able to communicate with developers, interpret Search Console data, understand content quality, identify technical dependencies and explain why a recommendation matters commercially.

From working across local businesses, ecommerce websites, travel and hospitality projects, I have found that SEO problems rarely exist in isolation. A page that is not ranking might have a content problem, but it could also have weak internal links, unclear intent, poor indexing, a canonical issue or simply be targeting a query that does not match what the business actually sells. The job is to diagnose the relationship between those problems rather than immediately publish another article.

That is why I would encourage a business owner to ask a potential SEO consultant in Kathmandu or elsewhere in Nepal to explain their reasoning using your actual website. Ask them which pages they would prioritise and why. You will learn much more from that discussion than from a generic presentation filled with screenshots.

Relevant experience also means knowing when not to do something. Not every page needs a schema type. Not every website needs hundreds of backlinks. Not every keyword deserves a dedicated article. Not every technical warning deserves immediate developer time. Good SEO involves prioritisation as much as implementation.

A Simple SEO Hiring Checklist for Nepali Businesses

Before hiring an SEO freelancer, consultant or agency, use this checklist:

  • Clear business objective
  • Relevant SEO experience
  • Technical SEO capability
  • Search-intent understanding
  • Keyword-to-page mapping
  • Content strategy
  • Local SEO knowledge where required
  • Transparent reporting
  • Verifiable case studies
  • Clear deliverables
  • Client owns important accounts
  • No ranking guarantees
  • Clear communication process
  • Defined measurement framework
  • Clear contract and scope

If a provider satisfies these points, you still need to judge the relationship itself. Can they explain complicated issues in language your team understands? Do they listen before recommending work? Do they distinguish between urgent problems and optional improvements? Do their reports help you make decisions?

The checklist is not intended to produce a perfect numerical score. It is a conversation tool. Two providers may both satisfy most of these requirements while offering very different approaches, and your decision should depend on your website, resources and business goals.

These approaches overlap substantially and should not be treated as replacements for a technically sound, useful website.
